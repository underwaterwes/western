## 3) Interviewer Prep Pack

## 3.1 30-Second Intro (Memorize)
“I’m a relationship-driven fundraiser/operator who combines donor-centered storytelling with disciplined pipeline execution. I’ve built portfolios by prioritizing qualification, strategic asks, and high-quality follow-up, while using Salesforce to keep forecasting and collaboration strong. I’m excited about your mission and see a direct fit between your campaign priorities and the way I drive donor movement from first touch to long-term stewardship.”

## 3.2 90-Second “Why This Role / Why This Foundation?”
“I’m drawn to this role because it sits at the intersection of mission impact and execution discipline. Your priorities — campaign growth, donor engagement, and measurable outcomes — align with how I work. I’m strongest when I can pair thoughtful donor strategy with clear systems: qualifying prospects, planning moves, and using Salesforce to keep everyone aligned on next steps. I also value cross-functional collaboration, because great fundraising depends on credibility across Programs, Finance, and leadership. This role looks like an opportunity to both accelerate near-term fundraising results and help build long-term donor trust.”

## 3.3 High-Probability Interview Questions + Strong Answer Frames
1. **“Tell me about yourself.”**  
   Frame: Present → past wins → why now. Include one donor win + one systems win.
2. **“How do you build a major gifts pipeline?”**  
   Frame: Segment → qualify → prioritize by propensity/affinity/readiness → move plans → review cadence.
3. **“How do you partner with leadership on asks?”**  
   Frame: briefing memo, role clarity, rehearsal, follow-up protocol.
4. **“How do you use Salesforce in day-to-day fundraising?”**  
   Frame: hygiene rules, stage definitions, dashboard usage, forecast discipline.
5. **“Describe a campaign challenge you solved.”**  
   Frame: Context → bottleneck → action → measurable result.
6. **“How do you handle donor objections?”**  
   Frame: listen, reframe to impact, offer options, preserve relationship.
7. **“How do you balance stewardship with new business?”**  
   Frame: calendar architecture + tiered touch model.

## 3.4 Questions You Should Ask Them
- “What pipeline metric most needs improvement in the first 6 months?”
- “How are Salesforce stages and forecast categories currently defined?”
- “Where does campaign momentum feel strongest, and where is it stalling?”
- “How do Programs and Development currently collaborate on impact reporting?”
- “What does great performance in this role look like by day 90 and by year 1?”

## 4) Salesforce Gap-Answer Bank (Ready-to-Use)

Use these if they ask about tooling depth, admin tasks, or gaps.

### 4.1 If You’re Strong User / Not Full Admin
“I use Salesforce daily for portfolio management, moves tracking, reporting hygiene, and forecast visibility. I’m not positioning myself as a platform architect, but I’m highly effective at translating fundraising needs into clear admin requests, validating outputs, and enforcing team usage standards. In prior roles, that has materially improved data quality and forecast confidence.”

### 4.2 If You’re New to NPSP / NPC Variant
“I have strong CRM discipline and transferable fundraising workflow expertise. While my direct experience in your exact Salesforce package is still growing, I ramp quickly by mapping stage definitions, required fields, key automations, and reporting logic in the first weeks. My goal is to be fully fluent in team-specific workflows fast, without compromising day-to-day productivity.”

### 4.3 If Asked About Dashboard/Reporting Depth
“I regularly use dashboards for pipeline coverage, stage velocity, ask outcomes, and retention signals. I’m comfortable with report logic and filters needed for frontline decisions, and I partner with admins/ops for advanced buildout when needed. I focus on turning reports into action, not just output.”

### 4.4 30-Day Salesforce Ramp Plan (Talk Track)
- Week 1: Data model + stage definitions + required hygiene fields.
- Week 2: Portfolio segmentation + moves cadence + baseline dashboard review.
- Week 3: Forecast assumptions + manager reporting alignment.
- Week 4: Quality audit + process improvements + training/standardization.

## 5) Donor / Campaign / Fundraising Notes

## 5.1 Donor Strategy Framework
- **Tier A (top prospects):** high-touch, leadership-involved, 12-month individualized plans.
- **Tier B:** structured quarterly cadence, thematic asks, upgrade pathways.
- **Tier C:** scalable stewardship + qualification triggers.

## 5.2 Campaign Talking Points (Customizable)
- Case for support in one sentence.
- Funding priorities with gift ranges and outcomes.
- Why now / urgency.
- Credibility proof points (impact metrics, leadership, governance).
- Stewardship promise post-gift.

## 5.3 Gift Conversation Structure
1. Discovery and values alignment.
2. Confirm interest area and decision process.
3. Present opportunity + impact outcomes.
4. Name a specific ask range.
5. Agree next step and follow-up date.

## 5.4 Metrics You Should Be Ready to Discuss
- Qualification rate
- Visit-to-ask conversion
- Ask-to-close conversion
- Average gift size / upgrade rate
- Retention by donor tier
- Forecast accuracy and stage velocity

## 6) 30/60/90-Day Plan (Interview-Ready)

### First 30 Days: Learn + Diagnose
- Absorb mission, team dynamics, and campaign priorities.
- Audit portfolio quality and pipeline stage integrity in Salesforce.
- Meet internal partners and align expectations.
- Deliver: diagnostic memo + top 20 prospect action plan.

### Days 31–60: Activate + Prioritize
- Launch move plans for priority prospects.
- Standardize follow-up and stewardship touchpoints.
- Tighten reporting rhythm and forecast definitions.
- Deliver: active pipeline review and early ask calendar.

### Days 61–90: Execute + Stabilize
- Advance key asks and close early opportunities.
- Validate dashboard usefulness with leadership.
- Propose 2–3 process improvements for durable gains.
- Deliver: quarter-end performance brief + year-ahead recommendations.
